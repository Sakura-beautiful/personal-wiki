Most personal websites for researchers follow the same pattern:

> About Me → Publications → Experience → Contact

This format works well for job applications, but it's optimized for a snapshot — it shows *who you are*, not *how you think*.

## The Problem with Portfolios

A portfolio is a curated highlight reel. It shows finished work, not the process. It doesn't capture:

- The papers you read and struggled to understand
- The experiments that failed and what you learned
- The debugging sessions that consumed three days
- The half-formed ideas that might become something

## A Wiki is Different

A wiki accumulates knowledge over time. It's not optimized for first impressions — it's optimized for **long-term thinking**.

The question I asked myself: *If I'm working on a problem in six months, what would I want past-me to have written down?*

That question shapes everything here.

## Structure

This site is organized around how I actually work:

- **Docs** — things I've understood well enough to explain systematically
- **Papers** — things I've read and my interpretation of them
- **Lab** — things I'm actively trying to understand
- **Projects** — things I've built
- **Blog** — thoughts that don't fit neatly elsewhere

## The Goal

Not to impress, but to accumulate. The goal is that in five years, this site is a useful record of how my understanding of robotics and AI developed.

*Things I learn, build, and understand.*
